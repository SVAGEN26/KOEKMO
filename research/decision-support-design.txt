MOTALA: AN IDEALIZED MODEL AND A DECISION-MAKER INTERFACE
======================================================
Design direction recorded 14 October 2026, implementation status updated
15 October 2026. The native M2 mechanism model is implemented; the interactive
decision workflow below remains proposed. No forecast superiority is established.

Purpose
-------
Help decision makers compare the timing, financial effects and service
consequences of explicit choices. Keep mechanisms small enough to explain.
Include a relationship when it matters to a decision and has a defensible
source or a clearly labelled assumption that can be tested.

Current integration
-------------------
The existing dashboard (../site/dist/app.js) runs the earlier model's JavaScript
engine. Its adapter generates Scenario extending Motala.Annual and offers optional browser
Modelica verification when compiler assets are installed. It is not an M1 UI.
MotalaResearch.Annual is the newer M1 model; motala_lab/modelica.py generates
its native Scenario and motala_lab/report.py creates a separate static report.
M1 has population and accounting components, but fiscal paths are statistical
inputs. It does not yet model service staffing or a debt-to-interest feedback.
Connecting a new control requires an implemented, tested mechanism behind it.

MotalaDynamics is the continuous M2 scenario model. It implements population,
staffing, capacity/construction, operating costs, approximate revenue and
debt-interest feedback, with an explicit pension cash bridge. Equations and
research provenance are in dynamics-equations.md. run_dynamics.py runs
the native model and exports annual integrals and end-of-year stocks with a
source snapshot. This report is separate from the dashboard. Service parameters
are illustrative; observed volumes, sector accounts and adjustment rates still
need estimation. M2 does not replace M1's historical evaluation results.

V2 adds separate sector allocations for each ledger category, optional staffing
and supplier limits, cash transition costs, and observed opening construction
spending. run_experiments.py now implements paired comparisons across explicit
sensitivity assumptions. Reports retain failed runs and compiler-resolved
parameters. The shared browser controls remain a separate integration task.

Component review, one at a time
------------------------------
1. Population: counts by age, births, deaths and migration. Use SCB and Motala
   observations. Finer ages are needed before attributing care needs to older
   groups. Migration is initially an external scenario; a municipal action
   should affect it only through a separately evidenced relationship.
2. Service demand: population times observed participation or care intensity.
   KI's March 2024 report motivates separating volumes from staffing and prices.
   Obtain local pupil counts and care volumes; population alone is insufficient.
3. Staffing and capacity: translate demand into required staff and facilities,
   then represent feasible adjustment over time. Starke motivates investigating
   persistent costs; estimate Motala's effects instead of importing coefficients.
4. Operating accounts: staff compensation, pensions, other costs and operating
   revenue, reconciled to sector accounts. Show fixed and variable costs.
5. Revenue: local tax base, tax rate, grants and equalisation. Use Swedish
   institutional sources and dated SKR inputs. Specify which inputs are external
   assumptions and which change in response to a simulated municipal decision.
6. Investment and financing: construction, commissioning, depreciation, cash,
   debt and interest. Preserve distinctions between expenditure and cash flows.
   Calgary informs the overall dynamic structure; Swedish accounts define it.

Cellier's paper informs the Modelica implementation. Annual accounts must remain
reconciled if continuous stocks and rates are introduced. In M1, net cost already
includes depreciation; replacing components must avoid counting it twice.

For each component, document its purpose, equations, units, time convention,
source and relevant page, parameter provenance, deliberate simplifications,
missing mechanisms, and observable quantities used for validation. Put concise
explanations beside equations and fuller notes in Modelica Documentation.

Decision-maker workflow
----------------------
Start with a named reference scenario and its observation date. Let the user
choose a concrete question, change a few meaningful inputs, and compare the
result with the reference across years. Proposed Swedish control groups:

  Beslut: staffing/service targets and an investment commissioning schedule.
  Omvärld: wage and price growth, interest and national tax-base scenarios.
  Modellantaganden: estimated service intensity, fixed costs and adjustment times.

Place municipal tax-rate choices under Beslut once the revenue mechanism is
implemented. Do not imply that national growth or migration can be commanded.
Adjustment time combines practical constraints and management choices; expose
an explicit implementation plan separately from the estimated response rate.

Each control needs a plain-language label, unit, baseline, effective year,
source or assumption status, and explanation of what it changes. Offer numeric
entry as well as sliders. A valid input range is not a confidence interval.
Policy targets do not imply instant achievement: show realized staffing and
capacity. Do not imply unchanged service quality from an unexplained saving.

Show annual result, cash, debt, service demand, available capacity and their
changes from the reference. Include a readable explanation of the main drivers.
Keep recorded outcomes, municipal budgets, forecasts and scenarios visibly
identified. Scenario ranges remain scenario ranges unless probability coverage
has been calibrated. Provide tables, accessible labels and exportable results.

Use progressive detail: an overview for a meeting, a component explanation for
discussion, and a source/equation view for scrutiny. The main view should make
the decision understandable without requiring knowledge of Modelica.

Shared computation and provenance
---------------------------------
Use one versioned scenario contract for the interface and simulation: base year,
horizon, dated observations, decisions, external paths and calibrated parameters.
Every editable field must map explicitly to a Modelica input or parameter.
Return results with the scenario identifier and model/data versions. Never show
results from an earlier simulation as if they reflect newly edited inputs.

Prefer a Modelica execution path as the authoritative scenario calculation.
If an immediate preview uses another engine, compare it with Modelica for
supported controls and label verification accurately. Export the scenario,
results and provenance together so a meeting's comparison can be reproduced.
M2 provides native parameter overrides and export provenance. The shared
interactive contract and browser integration are not implemented yet.

First complete increment
------------------------
Use education demand and staffing as the first small component to review:
  children -> enrolled pupils -> staff required -> staff available -> payroll.
Include facility capacity/cost where observations support it, and explicitly
represent purchased places or cross-boundary pupils where material. Reconcile
this component with the remaining net cost rather than adding it on top.

A first decision view can compare maintaining staffing per pupil with a changed
target, showing payroll, staffing and pupil implications over time. Estimate
response delays from evidence or mark them as sensitivity assumptions. Do not
invent a numerical effect on educational attainment or migration.

Before accepting the increment, check accounting, units, plausible limiting
cases, control-to-output behavior, and reproduction of the same scenario in
the UI and Modelica. Compare historical costs using only earlier information;
keep municipal budget and SKR comparisons aligned to their publication dates.
Ask decision makers to explain the difference between the scenarios and identify
the changed assumptions. Their ability to do this is an interface acceptance
criterion alongside the numerical checks.
